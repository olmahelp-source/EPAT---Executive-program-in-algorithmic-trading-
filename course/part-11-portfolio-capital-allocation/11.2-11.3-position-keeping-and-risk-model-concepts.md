## 476. Position keeping, accounting, P&L attribution

**Formulas** (average-cost accounting)
```
add to a position:   new_avg = (avg * |pos| + price * |qty|) / |new_pos|
reduce a position:   realized = closed_qty * (price - avg) * sign(pos)       avg unchanged
flip through zero:   close the whole old position, the remainder opens at the fill price
unrealized = pos * (mark - avg)
```

**Worked example.** One instrument, five fills:

| Fill | Realized | Position after | Average cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buy 100 @ 50 | 0 | 100 | 50.00 |
| Buy 50 @ 56 | 0 | 150 | 52.00 |
| Sell 120 @ 60 | 120 x (60 - 52) = **960** | 30 | 52.00 |
| Sell 100 @ 58 (closes 30, flips short 70) | 30 x (58 - 52) = **180** | -70 | 58.00 |
| Buy 40 @ 55 (covers 40 of the short) | 40 x (58 - 55) = **120** | -30 | 58.00 |

Total realized **1,260**. Marked at 54, the remaining short of 30 earns an unrealized **+120**.

**Code:** `t476_ledger` (class `Ledger`)
**Watch out:** the flip case is where ledgers go wrong. Test it explicitly. Match the broker's cost-basis convention (average or FIFO) before comparing.

## 477. Reconciliation with broker statements

**Method.** For each symbol in either book, classify the difference: missing at broker, unknown locally, or quantity mismatch beyond tolerance.

**Worked example.**

| Symbol | Local | Broker | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| AAA | 100 | 100 | match |
| BBB | -50 | -40 | **quantity mismatch** |
| CCC | 200 | 200 | match |
| DDD | 0 | (absent) | ignored (flat) |
| EEE | (absent) | 75 | **unknown locally** |

**Code:** `t477_reconciliation` (`reconcile`)
**Watch out:** run it every day and track the age of each break. A break that persists is usually a process error, not a rounding difference.

## 478. Risk models (Barra-style fundamental, statistical, and hybrid factor models)

**Formulas**
```
S = B F B' + D        B = factor loadings (assets x factors),  F = factor covariance,  D = diagonal specific variance
portfolio variance = (B'w)' F (B'w) + sum( w_i^2 * d_i )
                      systematic part     specific part
```

**Worked example.** Four assets, two factors (market vol 16%, value vol 8%, correlation -0.2), specific vols 10%, 12%, 15%, 8%, weights 30%, 30%, 20%, 20%.
- factor exposures: market **0.95**, value **0.13**
- systematic variance 0.02258, specific variance 0.00335, total 0.02593
- **87.1% of variance is systematic**, portfolio vol **16.1%**
- smallest eigenvalue of S = 0.0073 (positive, so the matrix is valid)

**Code:** `t478_risk_model`

## 479. Factor zoo problem and factor-crowding risk

**Method.** With m candidate factors, about `m x 0.0455` will show |t| above 2 by chance and `m x 0.0027` above 3. A Bonferroni threshold is `z(1 - 0.05 / (2m))`.

**Worked example.** 300 candidate factors, each pure noise, 240 monthly observations.
- |t| above 2: **19** factors (13.7 expected by chance)
- |t| above 3: **0** (0.8 expected)
- best |t| = 2.82; Bonferroni threshold for 300 tests = **3.76**

Nineteen "discoveries" at the usual cutoff, every one of them false.

**Code:** `t479_factor_zoo`
**Watch out:** require an economic reason and out-of-sample evidence for each factor, in addition to a stricter threshold.

## 480. Hedging (minimum-variance hedge ratio, beta, cross-hedging, basis risk)

**Formulas**
```
h* = Cov(spot, hedge) / Var(hedge) = rho * sigma_spot / sigma_hedge
hedge effectiveness = 1 - Var(spot - h*hedge) / Var(spot) = rho^2      (at the optimal h*)
```

**Worked example.** Spot vol 1.2%, hedge vol 1.0%, correlation 0.9 (20,000 days).
- theory h* = **1.08**, estimated **1.078**
- effectiveness at optimal h: **0.810** (theory rho^2 = 0.81); naive h = 1 gives 0.805
- estimating h from 60-day windows gives a 10th to 90th percentile range of **1.00 to 1.15**

Even a perfect hedge ratio removes only 81% of variance when basis risk remains (correlation 0.9), and the ratio itself wobbles by about plus or minus 0.07 from window to window.

**Code:** `t480_hedging`

## 481. Dollar-, beta-, and sector-neutral construction

**Formulas**
```
net dollar = sum of long weights - sum of short weights
net beta   = sum(w_long * beta_long) - sum(w_short * beta_short)
smallest-change neutralization:  w_new = w - A'(AA')^-1 A w,   A = [ones; beta]
```

**Worked example.** Three longs (40%, 30%, 30%, betas 1.2, 1.0, 1.4) and three shorts (same weights, betas 0.8, 0.7, 0.9).
- dollar neutral, but **net beta = +0.40**
- scaling the short book by 1.5 removes the beta, but then the book is **net short 50%**
- projecting onto both constraints at once gives weights 16.5%, 30.0%, -17.1% and -16.5%, 5.3%, -18.2%, both exposures zero, with gross exposure cut from 2.0 to 1.04

Fixing one exposure by scaling breaks another. Handle all constraints together in an optimizer.

**Code:** `t481_neutral_construction`
**Watch out:** the smallest-change projection can flip positions and shrink the book a lot, so use a proper constrained optimizer when it matters.

## 482. Tracking error, active share, and information ratio

**Formulas**
```
active weights a = w_portfolio - w_benchmark
active share = 0.5 * sum |a_i|
tracking error = sqrt( a' S a )
information ratio = expected active return / tracking error
```

**Worked example.** Portfolio 30/25/20/15/10, benchmark 40/20/15/15/10.
- active share **10%**
- tracking error **1.86%** (vols 18% to 30%, pairwise correlation 0.5)
- with a 1.5% expected active return: information ratio **0.81**

**Code:** `t482_active_measures`
**Watch out:** an active share of 10% means the portfolio is a near-index fund, whatever its label says.

## 483. Smart beta and index methodology

**Method.** Select by a score (top 30% of 100 stocks by value), then weight by market cap, equally, or by inverse volatility. Measure one-way turnover when the score updates (month-to-month score correlation 0.85).

**Worked example.**

| Weighting | Turnover per rebalance | Effective names | Largest weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Market cap | 32.5% | 16.9 | 12.4% |
| Equal | 33.3% | 30.0 | 3.3% |
| Inverse volatility | 34.9% | 26.6 | 6.2% |

Selection drives turnover (about a third of the book changes each rebalance whatever the weighting), and cap-weighting concentrates into about 17 effective names out of 30.

**Code:** `t483_smart_beta`

## 484. ESG integration and constraints

**Formulas** (minimum variance with budget and score constraints, shorts allowed)
```
w = S^-1 A' (A S^-1 A')^-1 b,    A = [ones; ESG scores],   b = [1; target score]
```

**Worked example.** Five assets with ESG scores 80, 60, 55, 70, 40.
- unconstrained minimum variance: score **71.7**, vol **10.249%**
- required score 70: already met (71.7), so the constraint is inactive and costs nothing
- required score **75**: vol **10.386%** (+0.14 points), weights 64.1, 22.6, 4.1, 14.2, -4.9
- required score **80**: vol **11.089%** (+0.84 points), weights include **negative** positions

Modest ESG targets are cheap, demanding ones are not.

**Code:** `t484_esg_constraint`
**Watch out:** the equality-constrained solution can short assets. A long-only minimum-variance problem with a score constraint needs a quadratic-programming solver.

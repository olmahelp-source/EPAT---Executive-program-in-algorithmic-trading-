## 485. Fixed fractional and fixed ratio sizing

**Formulas**
```
fixed fractional:  shares = equity * risk_fraction / (entry - stop)
```

**Worked example.** Equity 100,000, risk 1% per trade, entry 50, stop 48: shares = 100,000 x 0.01 / 2 = **500**.

Now simulate 2,000 accounts, 1,000 trades each: 45% winners paying +2R, 55% losers paying -1R (expectancy +0.35R per trade, Kelly fraction 0.175). Risking a fixed fraction of current equity per trade:

| Risk per trade | Median final equity (x start) | Median max drawdown | Probability of a 50% drawdown |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5% | 5.6 | 6.8% | 0% |
| 1% | 29.5 | 13.3% | 0% |
| 2% | 690 | 25.3% | 0.1% |
| 5% | 2.4 million | 54.0% | **68%** |
| 10% | 29 billion | 82.1% | **100%** |

The huge final-equity numbers at the bottom are not achievable in practice. They come with an almost certain 50% drawdown.

**Code:** `t485_fixed_fractional`

## 486. Kelly criterion, fractional Kelly, multi-asset Kelly

**Formulas**
```
binary bet, win probability p, payoff b to 1:    f* = (p b - (1 - p)) / b
continuous (excess return mu, volatility sigma): f* = mu / sigma^2        (leverage)
growth rate at leverage f:  g(f) = mu f - 0.5 sigma^2 f^2               maximum at f*: g* = mu^2 / (2 sigma^2)
```

**Worked example.**
- p = 0.55, b = 1: f* = **0.10**. p = 0.40, b = 2: f* = **0.10**.
- mu = 8%, sigma = 20%: Kelly leverage **2.0**, maximum growth **8% per year**.

Ten-year outcomes over 4,000 simulated paths:

| Leverage | Theory growth | Volatility | Median 10-year multiple | 5th percentile |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.25 x Kelly | 3.5% | 10% | 1.43 | 0.83 |
| 0.5 x Kelly | 6.0% | 20% | 1.85 | 0.62 |
| 1.0 x Kelly | **8.0%** | 40% | **2.31** | 0.26 |
| 1.5 x Kelly | 6.0% | 60% | 1.93 | 0.07 |
| 2.0 x Kelly | **0.0%** | 80% | 1.07 | 0.01 |

Half Kelly keeps 75% of the growth (6% of 8%) at half the volatility. If your edge estimate is twice the truth, "full Kelly" on that estimate is 2 x the true Kelly: growth zero.

**Code:** `t486_kelly`
**Watch out:** Kelly assumes you know mu. Use a fraction, and size for the estimated edge minus its uncertainty.

## 487. Volatility targeting and ATR-based sizing

**Formula**
```
position (leverage) = target_vol / forecast_vol           (cap the leverage)
forecast known at the start of the day, for example EWMA with lambda = 0.94
```

**Worked example.** 10 years of returns that alternate between calm half-years (10% annual vol) and turbulent half-years (40%). Target 10%, leverage capped at 3.

| | Calm periods | Turbulent periods |
|---|---|---|
| Unscaled realized vol | 10.2% | **39.4%** |
| Vol-targeted realized vol | 8.6% | **12.4%** |
| Average leverage | 0.80 | 0.30 |

Maximum drawdown: **74.6%** unscaled, **32.8%** vol-targeted. Realized vol runs a little under target in calm times because the forecast lags.

**Code:** `t487_vol_targeting`
**Watch out:** the forecast lags, so the first days of a volatility spike are still too big. Keep a leverage cap.

## 488. Risk-per-trade and stop-distance sizing

**Formulas**
```
position value = equity * risk / stop_distance_fraction
gap-adjusted:  position value = equity * risk / max(stop_distance, q99_overnight_gap)
```

**Worked example.** Equity 100,000, risk 1%, entry 50. The 99th percentile overnight loss from a fat-tailed gap distribution is **3.1%**.

| Stop | Stop distance | Position (% of equity) | Loss if a 10% gap goes through | Gap-adjusted position |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 48 | 4% | 25% | 2.5% of equity | 25% (no change) |
| 49 | 2% | 50% | 5.0% | **32.4%** |
| 49.5 | 1% | **100%** | 10.0% | **32.4%** |

A 1% stop sizes you to 100% of equity, and a single gap then costs 10%. The gap-adjusted rule caps the position where gap risk, not the stop, is the real risk.

**Code:** `t488_risk_per_trade`

## 489. Optimal f (Vince)

**Formula**
```
Terminal wealth multiple TWR(f) = product over trades of ( 1 + f * x_i / L ),    L = size of the worst loss
optimal f maximizes TWR(f)
```

**Worked example.** Ten trades (per-unit P&L): +150, -100, +200, -80, +120, **-150**, +90, +60, -50, +170.
- optimal f = **0.423**, TWR **1.78**
- f = 0.2: TWR 1.51; f = 0.5: **1.74**; f = 0.8: **0.89** (a loss)
- remove the worst trade (-150): the worst loss becomes -100 and optimal f jumps to **0.558**

Past the optimum, performance falls off a cliff, and the answer depends entirely on one trade.

**Code:** `t489_optimal_f`
**Watch out:** treat optimal f as an upper bound and use a fraction of it.

## 490. Equity-curve-based sizing and anti-martingale scaling

**Rule.** Trade at half size when the equity curve is below its 50-day average, full size otherwise.

**Worked example.** 300 simulated 2,000-day histories where the edge fades: strong for 750 days, negative for 750, positive again for 500.

| Sizing | Median final equity | Median max drawdown | 95th percentile max drawdown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Constant | 1.58 | 45.4% | 64.1% |
| Equity-curve rule | 1.49 | **32.9%** | **47.4%** |

The rule costs about 6% of final wealth and cuts typical and bad-case drawdowns by a quarter.

**Code:** `t490_equity_curve_sizing`
**Watch out:** the rule reacts after losses, so it works for regime-like decay and does little for sudden gaps.

## 491. Capital allocation across strategies (static, dynamic, performance-weighted)

**Worked example.** Three strategies with vols 8%, 12%, 16%, **identical true Sharpe of 0.8**, correlation 0.2; 10 years, 100 simulations, monthly rebalancing.

| Rule | Average Sharpe | Turnover per year |
|---|---|---|
| Equal capital | 1.09 | 0 |
| Inverse volatility | **1.12** | 0.01 |
| Performance-chasing (trailing 6-month Sharpe) | **0.90** | **2.43** |

When all strategies are equally good, chasing recent performance trades noise: it lowers the Sharpe by about 0.2 and turns over 243% of capital a year.

**Code:** `t491_strategy_allocation_rules`

## 492. Cash and margin buffer policy

**Formulas**
```
free margin = equity - margin_rate * notional
stressed free margin = (equity + loss) - stressed_margin_rate * stressed_notional
buffer needed = max(0, - stressed free margin)
```

**Worked example.** Equity 2.0M, notional 5.0M, margin rate 20% (free margin **1.0M**). Stress: an 8% price drop and a margin-rate hike to 35%.
- equity after the drop = 2,000,000 - 400,000 = 1,600,000; stressed margin = 35% x 4,600,000 = 1,610,000; stressed free margin = **-10,000** (a margin call)
- buffer needed to survive: **10,000** (about 0.5% of equity)

Probability of a margin call within a year (20% vol, 10% of paths see the rate hike):

| Notional | 4M | 5M | 6M | 7M |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Probability | 2.0% | 11.9% | 30.3% | 49.7% |

**Code:** `t492_margin_buffer`
**Watch out:** margin requirements change exactly when markets are stressed.

## 493. Pyramiding, scaling in and out

**Method.** Add a unit each time price rises 2, move every stop to 4 below the latest entry, and size each new unit so that the total open risk stays at the budget (1,000 on a 100,000 account).

**Worked example.**

| Add | Entry | Stop for all units | New units | Total units | Total open risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 100 | 96 | 250.0 | 250.0 | 1,000 |
| 2 | 102 | 98 | 125.0 | 375.0 | 1,000 |
| 3 | 104 | 100 | 187.5 | 562.5 | 1,000 |
| 4 | 106 | 102 | 281.2 | 843.8 | 1,000 |

Risk stays constant because rising stops lock in profit on the earlier units. Unit sizes are not monotone, and they would shrink if the stop trailed less tightly.

**Code:** `t493_pyramiding`

## 494. Leverage policy and margin-call avoidance

**Formula** (long position, leverage L = position / equity, maintenance margin m)
```
margin call when the price falls by   x = (1/L - m) / (1 - m)
```

**Worked example.** Maintenance margin 10%, asset with 20% annual volatility.

| Leverage | Price drop that triggers a call | Probability within one year |
|---|---|---|
| 2x | 44.4% | 0.1% |
| 3x | 25.9% | 7.7% |
| 5x | 11.1% | **43.7%** |
| 8x | 2.8% | **81.5%** |
| 10x | 0% (call at once) | certain |

**Code:** `t494_margin_call`
**Watch out:** margin calls force selling at the worst time. Leverage that is "safe on average" still fails in the tail.

## 495. Drawdown-dependent de-risking

**Rule.** Scale exposure by the current drawdown: 75% above 5%, 50% above 10%, 25% above 15%.

**Worked example.** 500 simulated 10-year histories of a strategy with 7% return and 15% vol.

| | Median final equity | Median max drawdown | 95th percentile max drawdown |
|---|---|---|---|
| No ladder | 1.76 | 31.2% | 50.3% |
| Ladder | 1.36 | **19.6%** | **25.6%** |

The ladder halves the bad-case drawdown (50.3% to 25.6%) and costs about 23% of median final wealth, because after a drawdown it also trades smaller during the recovery.

**Code:** `t495_drawdown_derisking`
**Watch out:** write the re-entry rule down. Without it the ladder never brings risk back up.

## 496. Fund-level mechanics (subscriptions, redemptions, NAV, fees, high-water mark)

**Formulas**
```
gross value = NAV_start * (1 + gross return)
performance fee = fee_rate * max(0, gross value - high_water_mark)
NAV after fee = gross value - fee;  high_water_mark = max(high_water_mark, NAV after fee)
```

**Worked example.** Start NAV 100, 20% fee, gross returns +20%, -10%, +15%.

| Year | Gross NAV | Fee | NAV after fee | High-water mark |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 120.000 | 4.000 | 116.000 | 116.000 |
| 2 | 104.400 | 0.000 | 104.400 | 116.000 |
| 3 | 120.060 | 0.812 | **119.248** | 119.248 |

Year 3 earns a fee only on the 4.06 above the old high-water mark, not on the whole 15.66 gain. Without a high-water mark (fee on every up year) final NAV would be **116.93**, so the investor would pay 1.68 more.

**Code:** `t496_fund_mechanics` (`nav_with_hwm`)

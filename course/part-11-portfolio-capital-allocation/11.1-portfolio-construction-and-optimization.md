## 462. Mean-variance optimization (Markowitz) and its instability

**Formulas**
```
maximize   mu'w - (lambda/2) * w'Sw      subject to   1'w = 1
solution   w = (1/lambda) * S^-1 (mu - gamma * 1)      gamma chosen so that the weights sum to 1
```

**Worked example.** Four assets with volatilities 15%, 18%, 22%, 12%, correlations between 0.2 and 0.8, expected returns 6%, 7%, 8%, 5%, risk aversion lambda = 4.
- mean-variance weights: **28.4%, 5.4%, 19.1%, 47.1%**
- minimum-variance weights (ignoring returns): 28.2%, 4.8%, 5.2%, 61.8%

Now add noise of 0.5 percentage points to each expected return (300 random draws) and re-optimize. The weights change by an average of **0.32** in L1 distance, so nearly a third of the portfolio moves because of a half-point change in a forecast that is certainly less accurate than that.

**Code:** `t462_mean_variance`
**Watch out:** the optimizer treats your estimates as exact and amplifies their errors.

## 463. Black-Litterman model

**Formulas**
```
equilibrium returns   pi = delta * S * w_market
view                  P * mu = q  with uncertainty Omega
posterior returns     mu_BL = [ (tau S)^-1 + P' Omega^-1 P ]^-1 [ (tau S)^-1 pi + P' Omega^-1 q ]
weights               w = (delta S)^-1 mu_BL
```

**Worked example.** Three assets, market weights 50%, 30%, 20%, delta = 2.5, tau = 0.05.
- implied returns: **7.70%, 9.19%, 3.37%**
- view: asset A outperforms B by 2% (the market currently implies A trails B by 1.49%), with confidence equal to the prior's
- posterior returns: **8.11%, 7.85%, 3.13%**; the A minus B spread moves from -1.49% to **+0.26%**
- resulting weights: **66.4%, 13.6%, 20.0%** against market weights 50/30/20

The view is blended with the prior, not adopted outright, so the tilt stays moderate and stable.

**Code:** `t463_black_litterman`
**Watch out:** the result depends on tau and Omega. Document how you chose them.

## 464. Risk parity and equal risk contribution

**Formulas**
```
risk contribution   RC_i = w_i * (S w)_i / sigma_p        (sums to sigma_p)
equal risk contribution:  RC_i = sigma_p / n for every asset
uncorrelated assets:      w_i proportional to 1 / sigma_i
```

**Worked example.** Volatilities 10%, 20%, 30%.
- uncorrelated: weights **54.5%, 27.3%, 18.2%** (exactly inverse volatility)
- with correlations 0.5, 0.2, 0.4: weights **55.5%, 25.3%, 19.2%**, each asset contributing **33.33%** of risk
- equal weights (33% each) would give risk contributions of **11.8%, 33.6%, 54.5%**: the 30% vol asset carries over half the risk

**Code:** `t464_risk_parity` (`erc_weights`)
**Watch out:** risk parity often needs leverage to reach a return target, and it concentrates in whichever asset has been calmest.

## 465. Hierarchical Risk Parity and HERC

**Method**
```
1. Distance d(i,j) = sqrt(0.5 * (1 - rho_ij)).
2. Cluster the assets (single linkage here) and order them so similar assets sit together.
3. Recursive bisection: split the ordered list in two, compute each half's variance with
   inverse-variance weights, and give the half with lower variance alpha = 1 - V_left / (V_left + V_right).
```
No matrix inversion is needed.

**Worked example.** Six assets in two clusters (low-vol cluster 10-12% vols, high-vol cluster 20-25% vols, within-cluster correlation 0.7, between 0.2). Leaf order: 2, 0, 1, 5, 3, 4.

| Method | Weights (asset 0 to 5) | In-sample vol |
|---|---|---|
| HRP | 26.0, 18.0, 36.3, 6.8, 5.6, 7.3 | 9.64% |
| Inverse variance | 31.9, 22.1, 26.3, 8.0, 6.6, 5.1 | 9.55% |
| Minimum variance (unconstrained) | 54.7, 6.2, 26.3, 11.1, 4.2, **-2.5** | 9.23% |

Minimum variance has the lowest in-sample vol by construction, yet it concentrates and goes short one asset. HRP's claim is stability out of sample, not a lower in-sample number.

**Code:** `t465_hrp` (`hrp_weights`, `single_linkage_order`)
**Watch out:** this version uses single linkage on plain distances for clarity. The published method clusters on a second-order distance, which changes the order in some cases.

## 466. Minimum variance and maximum diversification

**Formulas**
```
minimum variance:         w = S^-1 1 / (1' S^-1 1)
diversification ratio:    DR = (w' sigma) / sqrt(w' S w)          (weighted average vol / portfolio vol)
maximum diversification:  maximize DR  ->  w proportional to S^-1 sigma   (unconstrained)
```

**Worked example.** Four assets, vols 20%, 25%, 15%, 30%, correlations 0.2 to 0.5.

| Portfolio | Weights | Vol | DR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Equal | 25, 25, 25, 25 | 16.46% | 1.367 |
| Minimum variance | 24.2, 8.0, 65.1, 2.7 | **13.40%** | 1.299 |
| Maximum diversification | 19.2, 18.8, 44.8, 17.3 | 14.51% | **1.407** |
| Risk parity | 24.6, 20.4, 37.9, 17.1 | 14.86% | 1.402 |

**Code:** `t466_minvar_maxdiv`
**Watch out:** minimum variance loads on the lowest-vol asset (65% here) and concentrates in low-vol sectors.

## 467. CVaR and CDaR optimization

**Formulas**
```
CVaR_a(w) = average loss over the worst (1 - a) share of scenarios, loss = -R w
minimize CVaR_a(w) over long-only weights (solved here by projected subgradient; production code uses a linear program)
```

**Worked example.** Three assets over 5,000 days. A: 1.0% vol. B: a "calm" asset with 0.3% daily noise but a 1% chance each day of a -6% crash (total vol **0.70%**, the lowest). C: 1.2% vol.

| Portfolio | Weights | Vol | CVaR 95% | CVaR 99% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Minimum variance | 26.8, **54.7**, 18.5 | 0.523% | 1.351% | **3.315%** |
| Minimum CVaR (99%) | 47.3, **18.9**, 33.8 | 0.640% | 1.308% | **1.742%** |

Variance sees only B's low volatility and loads up on it. CVaR sees the crashes and cuts the weight to 19%, **halving the 99% tail loss** at the cost of 0.12 points of volatility.

**Code:** `t467_cvar`
**Watch out:** CVaR optimization needs enough tail scenarios. With 5,000 days and a 99% level, only 50 observations drive the answer.

## 468. Robust and resampled optimization

**Method (Michaud resampling)**
```
1. Estimate mu_hat and S_hat from the data.
2. Simulate many return samples from (mu_hat, S_hat), re-estimate and optimize each.
3. Average the optimal weights.
```

**Worked example.** Four assets, true Sharpe-like differences small, 60 days of data per estimate, 15 independent samples, risk aversion 4.

| | Plain optimizer | Resampled |
|---|---|---|
| Average standard deviation of each weight across samples | **0.39** | **0.21** |
| Average true utility | -0.0002 | **0.0118** |

True optimum utility is 0.0192. Resampling nearly halves the weight instability and lifts true utility from about zero to 61% of the optimum.

**Code:** `t468_resampled`
**Watch out:** this is a small experiment (15 samples), so treat the size of the improvement as indicative.

## 469. Factor-based construction (CAPM, Fama-French, APT, custom factors)

**Formulas**
```
factor regression     r_i = a_i + beta_i f + e_i
portfolio exposure    beta_p = sum w_i beta_i
neutralization        w_new = w - A'(AA')^-1 A w      with A = [1; beta] makes dollar and beta exposure zero
```

**Worked example.** Raw signal tilts 35%, 25%, 20%, 10%, 10% with betas 0.8, 1.0, 1.2, 0.6, 1.4 give a beta exposure of 0.97.
- after neutralizing both constraints: weights **13.5%, 5.0%, 1.5%, -13.0%, -7.0%**, net dollar 0, net beta 0

Estimating betas from 250 days (true betas 0.8, 1.0, 1.2, 0.6, 1.4): estimates **0.95, 1.04, 1.18, 0.52, 1.52** with standard errors about **0.08 to 0.09**.

**Code:** `t469_factor_construction`
**Watch out:** beta estimated from a year of daily data is uncertain by about 0.08, so the "neutral" book is only neutral up to that uncertainty.

## 470. Constraints (turnover, sector, leverage, cardinality, liquidity)

**Method.** Project onto the feasible set. For caps: weights `w_i = clip(v_i - tau, 0, cap)` with tau chosen so the weights sum to 1. A feasible solution needs `n x cap >= 1`.

**Worked example.** Five assets, vols 10%, 15%, 20%, 12%, 25%.

| Portfolio | Weights | Vol |
|---|---|---|
| Long-only minimum variance | 59.3, 9.9, 0, 30.9, 0 | 8.94% |
| Cap 30% per name | 30, 30, 10, 30, 0 | 9.82% |
| Cap 15% per name | **infeasible** (5 x 15% < 100%) | n/a |

Three caps bind in the second row, and the cap costs 0.88 points of volatility.

**Code:** `t470_constraints`
**Watch out:** check feasibility first and report which constraints bind, so users understand why the solver returned what it did.

## 471. Transaction-cost-aware optimization

**Formulas** (one asset, linear cost c per unit traded)
```
utility    u(w) = mu w - (lambda/2) sigma^2 w^2 - c |w - w0|
target     w_t = mu / (lambda sigma^2)
no-trade band half-width = c / (lambda sigma^2)
if w_t > w0 + band: w* = w_t - band;  if w_t < w0 - band: w* = w_t + band;  otherwise w* = w0
```

**Worked example.** mu = 6%, sigma = 20%, lambda = 3, cost 0.1%: target weight 0.50, band half-width **0.0083**.

| Current w0 | Optimal w* (formula) | Grid search |
|---|---|---|
| 0.30 | 0.4917 | 0.4917 |
| 0.495 | 0.495 (no trade) | 0.495 |
| 0.505 | 0.505 (no trade) | 0.505 |
| 0.60 | 0.5083 | 0.5083 |

The optimum trades only to the edge of the band, not to the target.

**Code:** `t471_tcost_aware`

## 472. Multi-period and dynamic optimization

**Method.** Backward induction on a grid of positions:
```
V2(w1) = max over w2 of [ u2(w2) - cost(w2 - w1) ]
V1(w0) = max over w1 of [ u1(w1) - cost(w1 - w0) + V2(w1) ]
```

**Worked example.** Expected return falls from 8% (period 1) to 2% (period 2), sigma 20%, lambda 3, quadratic cost 0.05 x (change)^2, start flat. Per-period targets: 0.667 then 0.167.

| Approach | Period-1 weight | Period-2 weight | Total utility |
|---|---|---|---|
| Myopic (ignores next period) | 0.36 | 0.25 | 0.015189 |
| Dynamic | **0.32** | 0.24 | **0.015360** |

The dynamic policy builds less in period 1 because it knows it will have to unwind.

**Code:** `t472_multiperiod`

## 473. Strategy-level allocation (portfolio of strategies)

**Idea.** Treat each strategy's return stream as an asset. Estimate covariances, then allocate by equal capital, Sharpe, or risk parity. Stress-test the correlations.

**Worked example.** Three strategies with vols 10%, 15%, 8% and Sharpe ratios 1.0, 0.6, 0.8. Backtest correlations 0.1 to 0.3, stress correlation 0.7 for every pair.

| Allocation | Weights | Vol (backtest corr) | Vol (stress corr) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Equal capital | 33.3 each | 7.72% | 9.88% |
| Sharpe-weighted | 41.7, 25.0, 33.3 | 7.28% | 9.49% |
| Risk parity | 35.6, 21.7, 42.7 | **6.98%** | **9.15%** |

Under stress correlations, portfolio volatility is 28% to 31% higher than the backtest suggested.

**Code:** `t473_strategy_allocation`

## 474. Capital allocation across asset classes and regimes

**Method.** Optimize per regime, then either blend by regime probability or switch. Add hysteresis (different on and off thresholds) to avoid flipping.

**Worked example.** Stocks and bonds. Calm regime (vols 15% and 5%, correlation -0.2, expected returns 8% and 3%): weights **58.9% / 41.1%**. Stress regime (vols 30% and 8%, correlation +0.3, expected returns -5% and 2%): **0% / 100%**.

Over 500 days with a noisy regime probability:

| Policy | Regime flips | Cumulative one-way turnover |
|---|---|---|
| Blend continuously by probability | n/a | 14.2 (1,420%) |
| Hard switch at 50% | 51 | not measured |
| Hysteresis (switch on above 70%, off below 30%) | **5** | **2.95** |

**Code:** `t474_regime_allocation`
**Watch out:** regime probabilities are noisy. Without a smoothing rule you will trade the noise.

## 475. Rebalancing policy (calendar, threshold, tactical)

**Idea.** Rebalance on a schedule or when weights drift outside a band. More frequent rebalancing cuts drift but costs more turnover.

**Worked example.** 60/40 stock/bond portfolio (stock 7% return and 16% vol, bond 3% and 5%), 20 years, 200 simulated paths, 10 bps cost on traded notional.

| Policy | Turnover per year | RMS drift from 60% | Avg final wealth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Never | 0% | **12.0 pts** | 2.84 |
| Monthly | 10.8% | 0.8 pts | 2.72 |
| Quarterly | 6.3% | 1.4 pts | 2.73 |
| 5-point band | **3.0%** | 2.1 pts | 2.74 |
| 2-point band | 7.0% | 0.9 pts | 2.73 |

The 5-point band delivers a tight portfolio with a third of monthly turnover. "Never" has the highest average wealth only because the stock weight drifts up and takes more risk, so compare risk as well as wealth. Costs at 10 bps are small here: the differences between the rebalanced rows are mostly risk, not cost.

**Code:** `t475_rebalancing`

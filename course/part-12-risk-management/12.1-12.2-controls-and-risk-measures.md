## 497. Pre-trade checks (position, exposure, fat-finger limits)

**Idea.** One risk gate that every order must pass. It returns all failing reasons, not only the first.
```
reject if  qty > max_qty  or  qty x price > max_notional
           |price - reference| / reference > collar     or  reference older than max_age
           |position + working + order| > max_position   (working orders count)
           orders in the last window >= rate limit
```

**Worked example.** Limits: quantity 1,000, notional 100,000, position 3,000, collar 5%, reference age 5 s, 3 orders per second.

| Order | Result |
|---|---|
| 1. Buy 500 @ 100.2, reference 100 | **accept** |
| 2. Buy 2,000 @ 100 | reject: quantity above limit, notional above limit |
| 3. Buy 800 @ 108 | reject: **price outside collar** (8% off) |
| 4. Buy 900, position already 2,500 | reject: projected position 3,400 above limit |
| 5. Buy 900, position 1,500 **plus 900 working** | reject: projected position 3,300 (ignoring working orders it would have passed at 2,400) |
| 6. Buy 100, reference price 10 s old | reject: **reference stale** |
| Burst of 5 orders in 0.4 s | first 3 accepted, orders 4 and 5 hit the **rate limit** |

**Code:** `t497_pretrade_checks` (`RiskGate`)
**Watch out:** apply the gate in every code path that can send an order. Test each rule with an order that breaks only that rule.

## 498. Real-time P&L and exposure monitoring

**Formulas**
```
unrealized P&L = qty x (price - cost)
gross exposure = sum |qty| x price          net exposure = sum qty x price
limit utilization = gross exposure / gross limit
```

**Worked example.** AAA: long 100 at cost 150, price 155 (1 s old). BBB: short 200 at cost 40, price 38.5 (**12 s old**).
- P&L: AAA **+500**, BBB **+300**, total **+800**
- gross exposure 23,200, net 7,800, utilization **46.4%** of a 50,000 limit
- BBB's price is flagged **STALE** (older than 5 s), so its +300 is not trustworthy

**Code:** `t498_pnl_monitor`
**Watch out:** a monitor that does not show data age can display a calm number while the market has moved.

## 499. Kill switches and circuit breakers

**Rules**
```
daily loss limit:    trip if P&L <= -2% of equity
reject-rate limit:   trip if more than 30% of the last 20 orders were rejected (more than 6)
effect:              block new orders, cancel working orders, alert; flatten only if configured
```

**Worked example.** Equity 100,000, so the loss limit is **-2,000**.
- a simulated bad day (drift -6 per minute, noise 40): the switch trips at **minute 276** with P&L **-2,027**
- an order stream of 10 accepted, 4 rejected, 6 accepted, 5 rejected: at order 22 the last 20 contain **7 rejects** (above the limit of 6), so it trips

**Code:** `t499_kill_switch` (`KillSwitch`)
**Watch out:** use integer counts for such tests. A rounded percentage comparison (35% vs 30% written as floating point) can fire at the wrong moment. Also test that the switch works when the component it monitors has failed.

## 500. VaR methods (historical, parametric, Monte Carlo) and VaR backtesting (Kupiec, Christoffersen)

**Formulas**
```
parametric VaR (normal):   VaR = z_a x sigma             z_0.99 = 2.326
historical VaR:            the 1% quantile of past losses
exceptions x out of T days, expected rate p = 1%
Kupiec:  LR = -2 ln[ (1-p)^(T-x) p^x ] + 2 ln[ (1-x/T)^(T-x) (x/T)^x ]        ~ chi-square(1), 5% critical value 3.84
Christoffersen independence: tests whether exceptions cluster (transition counts of hit / no hit)
```

**Worked example.** 2,500 simulated days with volatility clustering (GARCH 0.10 and 0.88); 99% VaR, 25 exceptions expected.

| Method | Exceptions | Kupiec LR | Kupiec p | Independence p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Normal, 250-day window | 34 | 2.94 | 0.086 | 0.49 |
| Historical, 250-day window | 39 | 6.76 | **0.009 (rejected)** | 0.64 |
| EWMA (lambda 0.94) | 31 | 1.35 | 0.245 | 0.40 |

All three under-cover (more exceptions than expected). The historical method is rejected outright because a 250-day window reacts slowly after volatility rises.

**Code:** `t500_var_backtest` (`kupiec`, `christoffersen_ind`)
**Watch out:** a single run is noisy. Backtest over many years and report the count, not just the pass/fail.

## 501. Expected Shortfall

**Formulas**
```
ES_a = average loss given that loss >= VaR_a
normal:  ES_a = sigma x phi(z_a) / (1 - a)
```

**Worked example.** Daily volatility 1%.

| Distribution | Level | VaR | ES | ES / VaR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Normal | 97.5% | 1.960% | 2.338% | 1.19 |
| Normal | 99% | 2.326% | 2.665% | 1.15 |
| Student-t (5 df) | 97.5% | 1.991% | 2.722% | **1.37** |
| Student-t (5 df) | 99% | 2.601% | 3.437% | **1.32** |

Fat tails leave VaR nearly unchanged at 97.5% but push ES up by 16%.

**Code:** `t501_expected_shortfall`

## 502. Scenario analysis and historical stress tests

**Method.** Revalue the book under shocks using sensitivities: P&L = sum of (sensitivity x shock).

**Worked example.** Sensitivities: equity 5,000,000 per 100% return, vega +8,000 per vol point, rates -12,000 per bp, credit -5,000 per bp.

| Scenario | Equity | Vol | Rates | Credit | **Total P&L** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Equity crash: -20%, vol +15, rates -50bp, credit +150bp | -1,000,000 | +120,000 | +600,000 | -750,000 | **-1,030,000** |
| Rates +100bp, equity -5% | -250,000 | +24,000 | -1,200,000 | -150,000 | **-1,576,000** |
| Credit crunch: credit +300bp, equity -12% | -600,000 | +80,000 | +300,000 | -1,500,000 | **-1,720,000** |
| Melt-up: equity +10%, vol -5 | +500,000 | -40,000 | -240,000 | +150,000 | **+370,000** |

The equity crash is the least harmful of the three bad cases because the rate and vega legs hedge part of it. The credit scenario is the worst, and a pure equity view would have missed it.

**Code:** `t502_scenarios`

## 503. Reverse stress testing

**Formulas** (linear P&L = g'x with risk factors x ~ N(0, S))
```
the most likely shock that produces loss L:   x* = - L S g / (g' S g)
distance in standard deviations:               d = L / sqrt(g' S g)
```

**Worked example.** Factors: equity return (daily vol 1.2%), rates (6 bp), credit (4 bp); correlations 0.3, -0.4, 0.2. Sensitivities as in topic 502. Daily P&L standard deviation = **90,155**.

| Loss target | Distance | Most likely shock (equity, rates, credit) | Normal probability |
|---|---|---|---|
| 200,000 | 2.2 sigma | -1.37%, +8.6bp, +5.7bp | 1.3% |
| 300,000 | 3.3 sigma | -2.06%, +12.8bp, +8.6bp | 0.044% |
| 500,000 | 5.6 sigma | -3.43%, +21.4bp, +14.4bp | essentially 0 |

The answer is a story: a 2% equity fall together with a 13 bp rate rise and wider credit is a 3.3-sigma day that costs 300,000. Ask whether that story is plausible.

**Code:** `t503_reverse_stress`
**Watch out:** the normal probability is a lower bound in fat-tailed markets. Treat the shock vector, not the probability, as the output.

## 504. Drawdown control and loss-limit rules

**Rule.** At 10% drawdown trade half size, at 15% halt for 20 days, return to full size once the drawdown is under 5%. After a halt, re-baseline the peak.

**Worked example.** 400 simulated five-year histories (mean 0.04% per day, vol 1%).

| Policy | Median final equity | Median max drawdown | 95th percentile max drawdown |
|---|---|---|---|
| No limits | 1.62 | 23.0% | 36.9% |
| Limits with a restart rule | 1.44 | 18.8% | 30.0% |
| Limits **without** a restart rule | 1.18 | 15.1% | 15.6% |

Without a restart rule, **72.5% of paths end stuck in a halt**: equity stays flat while the drawdown reference does not move, so the halt condition never clears. A limit with no defined re-entry is a permanent shutdown. With a restart rule the policy halts about 0.24 times per year.

**Code:** `t504_loss_limits` (`run_limits`)

## 505. Correlation breakdown and tail risk

**Formulas** (N equal-weight assets, equal vol sigma, pairwise correlation rho)
```
portfolio variance = sigma^2 x [ 1/N + (1 - 1/N) x rho ]
diversification ratio = sigma / portfolio vol
effective number of independent bets = 1 / [ 1/N + (1 - 1/N) x rho ]
```

**Worked example.** Ten assets, 20% vol each.

| Correlation | Portfolio vol | Diversification ratio | Effective independent bets |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 6.32% | 3.16 | 10.0 |
| 0.3 | 12.17% | 1.64 | 2.7 |
| 0.5 | 14.83% | 1.35 | 1.8 |
| 0.8 | 18.11% | 1.10 | **1.2** |
| 1.0 | 20.00% | 1.00 | 1.0 |

If correlations rise from 0.3 (calm) to 0.8 (crisis), portfolio volatility rises by half, from 12.2% to 18.1%.

**Code:** `t505_correlation_breakdown`

## 506. Liquidity risk and market-impact risk

**Formulas**
```
days to liquidate = position / (participation rate x average daily volume)
estimated cost = half spread + c x daily vol x sqrt(position / ADV)        (c = 0.5 here)
```

**Worked example.** Price 50, participation 10%.

| Name | Position value | Days to exit | Half spread | Impact | Total cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A (200k shares, ADV 2M, vol 1.5%) | 10.0M | 1.0 | 2.5 bp | 23.7 bp | 26,217 |
| B (500k shares, ADV 1M, vol 2.5%) | 25.0M | **5.0** | 7.5 bp | **88.4 bp** | **239,721** |
| C (50k shares, ADV 5M, vol 1.0%) | 2.5M | 0.1 | 1.5 bp | 5.0 bp | 1,625 |

Position B takes five days to exit and costs about 0.96% of its value, which an end-of-day mid-price mark never shows.

**Code:** `t506_liquidity`

## 507. Counterparty, settlement, and exchange risk

**Formulas**
```
expected loss = sum of probability of failure x loss given failure x unprotected balance
worst single failure = max over brokers of loss given failure x unprotected balance
```

**Worked example.** 10M split across three brokers with annual failure probabilities 0.3%, 0.5%, 1.5%, 25% loss given failure, 500k protected per broker.

| Allocation | Expected annual loss | Worst single failure | Herfindahl |
|---|---|---|---|
| All in broker 1 | 7,125 | 2,375,000 | 1.00 |
| 60 / 30 / 10 | 9,125 | 1,375,000 | 0.46 |
| Equal thirds | 16,292 | **708,333** | 0.33 |
| All in broker 3 | 35,625 | 2,375,000 | 1.00 |

Spreading across brokers reduces the worst case by about 70% but raises expected loss, because weaker brokers are now used. The trade-off is explicit.

**Code:** `t507_counterparty`

## 508. Model risk and parameter uncertainty

**Worked example.** True returns are Student-t (5 df), 1% vol. True 99% VaR = **2.607%**. Estimate it from 250 days, 5,000 times.

| Estimator | Average estimate | Relative spread (sd / mean) | 5th to 95th percentile |
|---|---|---|---|
| Historical quantile | 2.501% | **15.8%** | 1.95% to 3.21% |
| Normal (z x std) | 2.323% | 8.5% | 2.05% to 2.65% |

The normal estimate is **10.9% too low on average**, and tightly clustered around the wrong answer. The historical estimate is only 4% low on average, but its 90% band is 1.3 points wide, about half the VaR itself.

**Code:** `t508_model_risk`
**Watch out:** report a VaR with its uncertainty band, and keep a second, different model as a challenger.

## 509. Concentration and factor-exposure risk

**Formulas** (Meucci's effective number of bets)
```
Express the portfolio in the eigenbasis of S:  exposure e_i = (E'w)_i,   risk share p_i = e_i^2 lambda_i / (w'Sw)
ENB = exp( - sum p_i ln p_i )
```

**Worked example.** Ten equal positions (10% each), 20% vol each, three clusters (sizes 4, 3, 3), within-cluster correlation 0.8.

| Correlation structure | Effective number of bets | Portfolio vol | Share of risk in the largest component |
|---|---|---|---|
| Between clusters 0.2 | **1.07** | 13.0% | 98.8% |
| Between clusters 0.0 | 2.89 | 10.8% | 46.6% |
| All assets independent | 10.00 | 6.3% | 10.0% |

Ten positions (1 / sum of squared weights = 10 "effective names") can be one bet.

**Code:** `t509_concentration` (`enb`)

## 510. Risk budgeting and risk attribution

**Formulas**
```
RC_i = w_i (S w)_i / sigma_p          sums to sigma_p
risk-budget weights: iterate w_i <- w_i x sqrt(b_i / RC_i%) and renormalize, until RC% = b
```

**Worked example.** Three assets, vols 10%, 20%, 30%.
- capital weights 50% / 30% / 20% give portfolio vol **12.48%** and risk shares **25.7% / 38.1% / 36.2%**
- a target risk budget of 60% / 25% / 15% needs weights **71.9% / 18.9% / 9.3%**, vol **10.31%**, risk shares exactly 60 / 25 / 15

**Code:** `t510_risk_budgeting`

## 511. Greeks-based risk for derivatives books

**Formulas**
```
book Greek = sum of (units x Greek of each option)
delta-gamma-vega approximation:  P&L ~ delta dS + 0.5 gamma dS^2 + vega dSigma
```

**Worked example.** Spot 100, vol 20%, rate 3%. Book: **+200 calls** (strike 100, 3 months), **-300 puts** (strike 95, 3 months), **+100 calls** (strike 110, 6 months). Book value 632.
- delta **219.4** shares, gamma **0.645**, vega **15.69** per vol point, theta **-2.11** per day

| Shock | Full revaluation | Delta-gamma-vega | Error |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spot -10%, vol +5 | -2,323 | -2,084 | **-240** |
| Spot -5%, vol 0 | -1,103 | -1,089 | -13 |
| Spot +5%, vol 0 | +1,116 | +1,105 | +11 |
| Spot +10%, vol +5 | +2,473 | +2,305 | **+168** |

The approximation is accurate for small moves and misses 10% of the loss in the large combined shock. Use full revaluation for stress tests.

**Code:** `t511_option_book_risk` (`bs_greeks`, `bs_price` in `common.py`)

## 512. Gap, overnight, and weekend risk

**Formulas**
```
maximum position (fraction of equity) = risk budget / q, where q = the chosen quantile of overnight loss
```

**Worked example.** Overnight returns: normal noise (0.5% vol) plus a 1% chance of a jump averaging -3%.
- loss quantiles: 95% **0.85%**, 99% **1.39%**, 99.9% **5.63%**
- probability of a gap loss beyond a 2% stop: 0.67%; average loss when it happens: **4.06%**
- position cap for a 1%-of-equity gap budget: **71.7%** of equity using the 99% quantile, but only **17.8%** using the 99.9% quantile

The 99% quantile does not see the jumps at all. The jump risk lives beyond it.

**Code:** `t512_gap_risk`

## 513. Operational risk and fat-finger scenarios

**Method.** Frequency x severity simulation: events per year ~ Poisson(lambda), each loss ~ lognormal. Controls reduce frequency or cap severity.

**Worked example.** lambda = 4 events per year, median loss 10,000, log-sigma 1.5 (200,000 simulated years).

| | Mean annual loss | 95th pct | 99th pct | 99.9th pct |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| No controls | 123,441 | 395,076 | 807,875 | **2,008,854** |
| Controls: half the frequency, 50,000 hard cap per event | 34,734 | 102,243 | 143,111 | **195,829** |

Controls cut the average loss by 72% and the worst-case year tenfold, mostly because the hard cap removes the heavy tail.

**Code:** `t513_operational_risk`

## 514. Risk of ruin

**Method.** Simulate paths where each trade wins or loses a fixed fraction f of equity; ruin means falling to 50% of starting equity at any point in 1,000 trades. Win probability 0.55, 1:1 payoff.

**Worked example.**

| Risk per trade | Ruin probability, edge known | Ruin probability, win rate unknown (uniform 0.48 to 0.58) |
|---|---|---|
| 2% | 0.1% | **17.1%** |
| 5% | 11.5% | 42.3% |
| 10% | 46.5% | 68.8% |
| 20% | 90.3% | 92.2% |

If you are unsure about your edge, 2% per trade goes from nearly safe (0.1%) to a one-in-six chance of halving the account.

**Code:** `t514_risk_of_ruin`
